# Explicit-Step Planner Inability Testing Plan

## Change Summary

Prove that a valid step declaration fails the actual assignment or synthesis, preserves one canonical failure and exact reason, and propagates through the existing caller boundaries. Prove that an ordinary failed unit settles all admitted members before choosing an authoritative primary failure. Test at the parser, step engine, and production provider/Java boundaries where each behavior is observable.

## Impacted Areas and Risks

- Step action protocol and correction (`StepAction`, `StepActionValidator`, `StepActionCorrection`, `StepPromptBuilder`, and the step engine). A tolerant mapper could accept a coercion or silently discard extra fields. A global strictness change could reject an existing successful action or bare result.
- Explicit-step execution and `MissionLifecycle`. Incorrect ordering could hide a terminal or cancellation cause, drop or interrupt queued work, or publish a success for failed work. Incorrect placement could accept the declaration after a quota or deadline failure, consume an extra slot, or trigger output correction.
- `ExecutionBinding`, `LoomspanSession`, and standard tool feedback. Incorrect attribution could record the wrong frame or duplicate the failure. Lossy reserialization could change the signal. Shared subtype handling must preserve Java-origin meaning and normal standard tool behavior.
- Protected application contracts are `SkillTemplate` and admitted Java execution, exact `PlannerInabilityException` inheritance and message, and ordinary successful outputs and configuration. The old first-any-failure propagation rule is intentionally replaced. Canonical diagnostic validation covers current-run records. Cross-component presentation, structured chat readers, and complete release-fixture verification belong to PR 29.3.
